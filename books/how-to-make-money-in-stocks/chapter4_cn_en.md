# Chapter4 A = Annual Earnings Increases: Look for Big Growth

> 第一部分 · Part 1


> **中文标题：** A = 年度盈利增长：寻找大幅增长

**中文翻译（导语）**

任何公司偶尔都能报出一个好的季度盈利。而且如我们所见，强劲的当前季度盈利对于挑选市场多数最大赢家至关重要。但这还不够。

为了确保最新业绩不是昙花一现、你所研究的公司确实质量上乘，你必须要求更多证据。办法就是审视公司的年度盈利增长率。

要寻找在过去三年中每年每股收益都有所增长的股票。通常你不想看到第二年的盈利下滑，即便次年业绩回升到历史最高水平。正是最近几个季度强劲盈利，加上近年来稳健增长的记录，共同造就了一只绝佳的股票，或至少是一只在大盘上升趋势中成功概率更高的股票。

**英文原文（导语）**

Any company can report a good earnings quarter every once in a while. And as we’ve seen, strong current quarterly earnings are critical to picking most of the market’s biggest winners. But they’re not enough. 

To make sure the latest results aren’t just a flash in the pan, and the company you’re looking at is of high quality, you must insist on more proof. The way to do that is by reviewing the company’s annual earnings growth rate. 

Look for annual earnings per share that have increased in each of the last three years. You normally don’t want the second year’s earnings to be down, even if the results in the following year rebound to the highest level yet. It’s the combination of strong earnings in the last several quarters plus a record of solid growth in recent years that creates a superb stock, or at least one with a higher probability of success during an uptrending general market. 

### Select Stocks with 25% to 50% and Higher Annual Earnings Growth Rates

你所选公司的年盈利增长率应当是 25%、50% 甚至 100% 或更多。1980 到 2000 年间，我们研究的全部知名股票在其早期崭露阶段的中位年增长率是 36%。四分之三的大赢家在股价大涨之前的三年、有些情况下是五年里，至少显示出一定的正年增长。

一只股票在启动之前五年，典型的每股收益递增过程可能类似：0.70、1.15、1.85、2.65 和 4.00 美元。

少数情况下，如果随后一年的盈利回升到新高，你也可以接受五年中有一年下滑。

一家公司可能一年每股赚 4.00 美元，次年 5.00 美元，再下年 6.00 美元，然后跌到每股 3.00 美元。如果下一份年报是每股 4.00 美元、而前一年是 3.00 美元，尽管同比有 33% 的增长，这也不是一份好报告。它之所以看起来正面，唯一原因是前一年的盈利（每股 3.00 美元）被压得太低，任何改善都显得不错。要点在于：利润只是在缓慢恢复，且仍远低于该公司每股 6.00 美元的盈利高峰。

分析师对下一年盈利的一致预期也应向上，越高越好。但请记住：预测只是个人意见，意见可能出错（过高或过低）。实际报告的盈利才是事实。

The annual rate of earnings growth for the companies you pick should be 25%, 50%, or even 100% or more. Between 1980 and 2000, the median annual growth rate of all outstanding stocks in our study at their early emerging stage was 36%. Three out of four big winners showed at least some positive annual growth over the three years, and in some cases the five years, preceding the stocks’ big run-ups. 

A typical earnings per share progression for the five years preceding the stock’s move might be something like \$0.70, \$1.15, \$1.85, \$2.65, and \$4.00. 

In a few cases, you might accept one down year in five as long as the following year’s earnings move back to new high ground. 

It’s possible a company could earn \$4.00 a share one year, \$5.00 the next, \$6.00 the next, and then \$3.00 a share. If the next annual earnings statement was, say, \$4.00 per share versus the prior year’s \$3.00, this would not be a good report despite the 33% increase over the prior year. The only reason it might seem positive is that the previous year’s earnings (\$3.00 a share) were so depressed that any improvement would look good. The point is, profits are recovering slowly and are still well below the company’s peak annual earnings of \$6.00 a share. 

The consensus among analysts on what earnings will be for the next year should also be up—the more, the better. But remember: estimates are personal opinions, and opinions may be wrong (too high or too low). Actual reported earnings are facts. 

### Look for a Big Return on Equity

你还应了解另外两项衡量盈利能力和增长的指标：净资产收益率与每股现金流。

净资产收益率（ROE）用净利润除以股东权益计算。它显示公司运用资金的效率，从而有助于把管理良好的公司与经营不善的公司区分开来。我们的研究显示，过去 50 年里几乎所有最伟大的成长股，其 ROE 都至少达到 17%。（真正卓越的成长型公司会显示出 25% 到 50% 的 ROE。）

要确定现金流，可把公司列示的折旧金额加回来，以反映内部产生的现金量。一些成长股的年度每股现金流，还可能比实际的每股收益高出至少 20%。

You should also be aware of two other measurements of profitability and growth: return on equity and cash flow per share. 

Return on equity, or ROE, is calculated by dividing net income by shareholders’ equity. This shows how efficiently a company uses its money, thereby helping to separate well-managed firms from those that are poorly managed. Our studies show that nearly all the greatest growth stocks of the past 50 years had ROEs of at least 17%. (The really superior growth situations will sport 25% to 50% ROEs.) 

To determine cash flow, add back the amount of depreciation the company shows to reflect the amount of cash that is being generated internally. Some growth stocks can also show annual cash flow per share that is at least 20% greater than actual earnings per share. 

### Check the Stability of a Company’s Three-Year Earnings Record

通过研究，我们确定了另一个被证明对挑选成长股很重要的因素：过去三年年度盈利增长的稳定性与一致性。我们的稳定性指标以 1 到 99 的刻度表示，计算方法与多数统计不同。数字越低，过去的盈利记录越稳定。计算方法是：把过去三或五年的季度盈利制成图，围绕标注点拟合一条趋势线，以确定偏离基本增长趋势的程度。

盈利平稳的成长股，其稳定性数值往往低于 20 或 25。稳定性评级超过 30 的公司更具周期性，其增长也略欠可靠。在其他条件相同的情况下，你可能希望寻找过去盈利增长更具可持续性、一致性和稳定性的股票。一些每年增长 25% 的公司，稳定性评级可能只有 1、2 或 3。把几年的季度盈利标注在对数刻度图上时，盈利线应近乎笔直、持续向上。多数情况下，最近几个季度的增速会有所加快。

（示例：盈利稳定性）

盈利稳定性数值习惯上紧跟在公司年增长率之后显示，但多数分析师和投资服务机构懒得做这项计算。我们在许多机构产品中显示它们，也在为个人投资者设计的 Daily Graphs 和 Daily Graphs Online 中显示。

Through our research, we’ve determined another factor that has proved important in selecting growth stocks: the stability and consistency of annual earnings growth over the past three years. Our stability measurement, which is expressed on a scale of 1 to 99, is calculated differently from most statistics. The lower the figure, the more stable the past earnings record. The figures are calculated by plotting quarterly earnings for the past three or five years and fitting a trend line around the plotted points to determine the degree of deviation from the basic growth trend. 

Growth stocks with steady earnings tend to have a stability figure below 20 or 25. Companies with stability ratings over 30 are more cyclical and a little less dependable in terms of their growth. All other things being equal, you may want to look for stocks showing a greater degree of sustainability, consistency, and stability in past earnings growth. Some companies that are growing 25% per year could have a stability rating of 1, 2, or 3. When the quarterly earnings for several years are plotted on a log-scale chart, the earnings line should be nearly straight, consistently moving up. In most cases there will be some acceleration in the rate of increase in recent quarters.

If you restrict your stock selections to ventures with proven growth records, you will avoid the hundreds of investments with erratic histories or cyclical recoveries in profits. A few such stocks could “top out” as they approach the peaks of their prior earnings cycle. 

### What Is a Normal Stock Market Cycle?

历史表明，多数牛市（上升市）持续两到四年，随后是衰退或熊市（下跌市），然后另一轮牛市开始。

在新牛市的起步阶段，成长股通常率先领涨并创出新高。这些公司的利润逐季增长，但股价此前被疲弱的大盘压制。大盘下跌与公司利润持续增长的组合，会把市盈率（P/E）压缩到对机构投资者有吸引力的水平——对他们而言，市盈率很重要。

钢铁、化工、造纸、橡胶、汽车、机械等基础行业的周期股，通常在新牛市早期阶段滞后。

年轻的成长股通常会在至少两轮牛市周期中占据主导。之后，焦点可能短期转向周期股、困境反转股或其他新近改善的板块。

过去四分之三的大赢家是成长股，四分之一是周期股或困境反转股。1982 年，克莱斯勒和福特就是两只这样的困境反转股。周期股与困境反转机会在 1953–1955、1963–1965 和 1974–1975 年的市场浪潮中领先。造纸、铝、汽车、化工和塑料等周期股在 1987 年重回前台，同样具周期性的住宅建筑股也在其他时期领先。困境反转的例子包括 1994 年的 IBM 和 2003 年的苹果。

不过，即便周期股当道，也仍有一些相当引人注目的年轻成长股可供选择。美国的周期股往往属于较老、效率较低的行业。其中一些公司原本并无竞争力，直到中国基础工业的迅速壮大推动了对钢铁、铜、化工和石油的需求。这就是为什么 2000 年熊市在 2003 年结束后，周期股被大举复兴。

然而它们仍是周期股，未必代表美国真正的未来。此外，美国大型老牌公司常常还有一个规模上的劣势：它们实在太庞大，无法持续创新、不断自我更新，以与灵活的外国对手和美国年轻的新企业家竞争。

周期股的上涨往往更短暂，一有衰退或盈利放缓的迹象就容易失去动能。如果你决定买入一只困境反转股，要寻找至少 5% 到 10% 的年度盈利增长，以及连续两个季度的急剧盈利复苏，使最近 12 个月的业绩达到或非常接近新高。查看股票图表上 12 个月的盈利线，盈利上冲的角度越陡越好。

如果利润上冲如此剧烈以致创出新高，有时一个季度的盈利反转就够了。为钢铁业供应铁矿石球团的 Cleveland Cliffs（现称 Cliffs Natural Resources），2004 年从亏损状态开始，季度盈利先是大幅加速 64%，随后又增长 241%。在这一推动下，股价在接下来八个月里迅速上涨 170%。

History demonstrates most bull (up) markets last two to four years and are followed by a recession or a bear (down) market. Then another bull market starts. 

In the beginning phase of a new bull market, growth stocks are usually the first to lead and make new price highs. These are companies whose profits have grown quarter to quarter, but whose stocks have been held back by the poor general market conditions. The combination of a general market decline and a stock’s continued profit growth will have compressed the price/earnings (P/E) ratio to a point where it is attractive to institutional investors, for whom P/Es are important. 

Cyclical stocks in basic industries such as steel, chemicals, paper, rubber, autos, and machinery usually lag in the new bull market’s early phase. 

Young growth stocks will typically dominate for at least two bull market cycles. Then the emphasis may change to cyclicals, turnarounds, or other newly improved sectors for a short period. 

While three out of four big market winners in the past were growth stocks, one in four was a cyclical or turnaround situation. In 1982, Chrysler and Ford were two such spirited turnaround plays. Cyclical and turnaround opportunities led in the market waves of 1953–1955, 1963–1965, and 1974–1975. Cyclicals including paper, aluminum, autos, chemicals, and plastics returned to the fore in 1987, and home-building stocks, which are also cyclical, have led in other periods. Examples of turnaround situations include IBM in 1994 and Apple in 2003. 

Yet even when cyclical stocks are in favor, some pretty dramatic young growth issues are also available. Cyclical stocks in the United States are often those in older, less-efficient industries. Some of these companies weren’t competitive until the demand for steel, copper, chemicals, and oil surged as a result of the rapid buildup of basic industries in China. That’s why cyclicals were resurrected aggressively after the 2000 bear market ended in 2003. 

They are still cyclical stocks, however, and they may not represent America’s true future. In addition, large, old-line companies in America frequently have the added disadvantage of size: they are simply too large to be able to innovate and continually renew themselves so that they can compete with nimble foreign rivals and with America’s young new entrepreneurs. 

Rallies in cyclical stocks may tend to be more short-lived and prone to falter at the first hint of a recession or an earnings slowdown. Should you decide to buy a turnaround stock, look for annual earnings growth of at least 5% to 10% and two straight quarters of sharp earnings recovery that lift results for the latest 12 months into or very near new high ground. Check the 12-month earnings line on a stock chart; the sharper the angle of the earnings upswing, the better. 

If the profit upswing is so dramatic that it reaches a new high, one quarter of earnings turnaround will sometimes suffice. Cleveland Cliffs, a supplier of iron ore pellets to the steel industry (and now known as Cliffs Natural Resources), came from a deficit position to dramatically accelerate quarterly earnings in 2004 by 64% and then by 241%. With that impetus, the stock rapidly advanced 170% in the next eight months. 

### How to Weed Out the Losers in a Group

坚持要求三年盈利增长，能帮你迅速剔除任何行业组中 80% 的股票。多数组中多数股票的增长率平淡无奇甚至不存在——与下面这些例子形成对照：

• 施乐，在 1963 年 3 月到 1966 年 6 月股价飙升 700% 之前，以 32% 的年率增长。

• 沃尔玛，在 1977 到 1990 年火箭式上涨 11200% 之前，持续创造 43% 的年增长率。

• 思科系统，其盈利在 1990 年 10 月以 257% 的速度爆发式增长；微软在 1986 年 10 月以 99% 的速度增长，都在各自的巨大上涨之前。

• Priceline.com，2004 到 2006 年间盈利从每股 96 美分增长逾一倍至 2.03 美元，随后在接下来五个季度里股价涨了两倍。

• 谷歌，2002 年每股盈利 55 美分，2004 年已扩张到每股 2.51 美元，随后股价在 2007 年前从 200 美元涨到 700 美元。

请记住，一份年度增长记录并不必然使一家公司成为稳健的成长股。事实上，一些所谓的成长股，其报告的增长比它们早期市场时期大幅放缓。一轮周期中的许多成长龙头，不会在下一轮周期中重演。

一家拥有 30% 出色三年增长记录、但最近几个季度盈利增长已放缓到 10% 或 15% 的公司，其表现就像一只完全成熟的成长股。较老、较大的组织通常以较慢的增长为特征，其中许多应当回避。美国不断被新的创新型企业家公司引领和驱动。正是它们，而非我们的政府，创造了我们的新产业。

Insisting on three years of earnings growth will help you quickly weed out 80% of the stocks in any industry group. Growth rates for most stocks in most groups are lackluster or nonexistent—unlike, for example, 

• Xerox, which was growing at a 32% annual rate before its shares soared 700% from March 1963 to June 1966 

• Wal-Mart Stores, which consistently created an annual growth rate of 43% before rocketing 11,200% from 1977 to 1990 

• Cisco Systems, whose earnings were exploding at a 257% rate in October 1990, and Microsoft, which was growing at a 99% clip in October 1986, before their enormous advances 

• Priceline.com, which from 2004 to 2006 more than doubled its earnings from 96 cents a share to \$2.03, before it tripled in price in the next five quarters 

• Google, which had already expanded its earnings from 55 cents a share in 2002 to \$2.51 a share in 2004 before its stock climbed from \$200 to \$700 by 2007 

Keep in mind that an annual growth record doesn’t necessarily make a company a solid growth stock. In fact, some so-called growth stocks report substantially slower growth than they did in earlier market periods. Many growth leaders in one cycle do not repeat in the next cycle. 

The stock of a company that has an outstanding three-year growth record of 30% but whose earnings growth has slowed to 10% or 15% in the last several quarters acts like a fully mature growth stock. Older and larger organizations are usually characterized by slower growth, and many of them should be avoided. America is continually led and driven by new innovative entrepreneurial companies. They, and not our government, create our new industries. 

### Insist on Both Annual and Current Quarterly Earnings Being Excellent

一只出众的股票，既需要近年来健康的增长记录，也需要最近几个季度强劲的当前盈利记录。正是这两个关键因素的强力结合——而非二者之一——造就了一只超级股票，或至少是一只真正成功概率更高的股票。

要找到一家当前盈利强劲且加速、同时拥有扎实三年增长的公司，最快捷的办法，是查看《投资者商业日报》研究股票表中每只股票所附的专有每股收益（EPS）评级。

A standout stock needs both a sound growth record in recent years and a strong current earnings record in the last several quarters. It’s the powerful combination of these two critical factors, rather than one or the other, that creates a super stock, or at least one that has a higher chance for true success. 

The fastest way to find a company with strong and accelerating current earnings and solid three-year growth is by checking the proprietary Earnings per Share (EPS) Rating provided for every stock listed in Investor’s Business Daily’s research stock tables. 

The EPS Rating measures a company’s two most recent quarters of earnings growth against the same quarters the year before and examines its growth rate over the last three years. The results are then compared with those of all other publicly traded companies and rated on a scale from 1 to 99, with 99 being best. An EPS Rating of 99 means a company 

### has outperformed 99% of all other companies in terms of both annual and recent quarterly earnings performance.

——其年度和近期季度盈利表现都优于其他 99% 的公司。

If the stock is newly issued and the company doesn’t have a three-year earnings record, look for big earnings increases and even bigger sales growth over the last five or six quarters. One or two quarters of profitability are often not enough and indicate a less-proven stock that might fall apart somewhere down the line. 

### Are Price/Earnings Ratios Really Important?

如果你和多数投资者一样，你大概学过：关于一只股票，你需要了解的最重要的事情就是它的市盈率。那么，请做好迎接一个击碎泡沫的意外的准备。

多年来，分析师一直把市盈率作为基本衡量工具，用来判断一只股票是被低估（市盈率低）、应当买入，还是被高估（市盈率高）、应当卖出。但我们对 1880 年至今最成功股票的持续分析显示，与多数投资者的认知相反，市盈率并不是价格变动的相关因素，与股票该买该卖也几乎无关。

我们发现，远为关键的，是每股收益的百分比增长。因为某只证券市盈率低、或处于其历史市盈率区间的低端，就说它“被低估”，可能是无稽之谈。首要考虑应当是：盈利的变化率是在显著上升还是显著下降。

1953 到 1985 年间，表现最好的股票在其早期崭露阶段的平均市盈率为 20。（同期道琼斯工业指数的平均市盈率为 15。）在上涨过程中，最大的赢家把市盈率扩张了 125%，达到约 45。1990 到 1995 年间，真正的龙头起始平均市盈率为 36，并扩张到 80 多。但这些只是平均值。多数大赢家的起始市盈率在 25 到 50 之间，市盈率扩张幅度在 60 到 115 之间。在 1990 年代末的市场狂热中，这些估值升到更高的水平。价值型买家错过了几乎所有这些了不起的投资。

If you’re like most investors, you’ve probably learned the most important thing you need to know about a stock is its P/E ratio. Well, prepare yourself for a bubble-bursting surprise. 

For years, analysts have used P/E ratios as their basic measurement tool in deciding whether a stock is undervalued (has a low P/E) and should be bought, or is overvalued (has a high P/E) and should be sold. But our ongoing analysis of the most successful stocks from 1880 to the present shows that, contrary to most investors’ beliefs, P/E ratios were not a relevant factor in price movement and have very little to do with whether a stock should be bought or sold. 

Much more crucial, we found, was the percentage increase in earnings per share. To say that a security is “undervalued” because it’s selling at a low P/E or because it’s in the low end of its historical P/E range can be nonsense. Primary consideration should be given to whether the rate of change in earnings is substantially increasing or decreasing. 

From 1953 through 1985, the average P/E ratio for the best-performing stocks at their early emerging stage was 20. (The average P/E of the Dow Jones Industrials over the same period was 15.) As they advanced, the biggest winners expanded their P/Es by 125%, to about 45. From 1990 to 1995, the real leaders began with an average P/E of 36 and expanded into the 80s. But these were just the averages. Beginning P/Es for most big winners ranged from 25 to 50, and the P/E expansions varied from 60 to 115. In the market euphoria of the late 1990s, these valuations increased to even greater levels. Value buyers missed almost all of these tremendous investments. 

### Why You Missed Some Fabulous Stocks!

这些发现强烈表明：如果你不愿以 25 到 50 倍、甚至高得多的市盈率买入成长股，你就自动排除了大部分最好的投资机会！你错过了微软、思科系统、家得宝、美国在线，以及许多许多其他公司在它们市场表现最出色时期的机会。

我们的研究提示，市盈率是盈利加速的终端结果，而盈利加速又吸引了大型机构买家，从而带来强劲的股价表现。市盈率不是出色表现的原因。例如，研究发现高市盈率因牛市而出现；低市盈率（周期股除外）通常因熊市而出现。

在火爆的牛市中，不要仅仅因为一只股票的市盈率看起来太高就忽视它。它可能是下一个大赢家。也绝不要仅仅因为市盈率看起来便宜就买入一只股票。市盈率低通常有充分的理由，而且没有哪条金科玉律能阻止一只以 8 到 10 倍市盈率交易的股票继续下跌到 4 到 5 倍。

许多年前，当我刚开始研究市场时，我以 4 倍市盈率买入 Northrop，然后难以置信地看着股价下跌到市盈率 2 倍。

These findings strongly suggest that if you weren’t willing to buy growth stocks at 25 to 50 times earnings, or even much more, you automatically eliminated most of the best investments available! You missed Microsoft, Cisco Systems, Home Depot, America Online, and many, many others during their periods of greatest market performance. 

Our studies suggest P/E ratios are an end effect of accelerating earnings that, in turn, attract big institutional buyers, resulting in strong price performance. P/Es are not a cause of excellent performance. High P/Es, for example, were found to occur because of bull markets. Low P/Es, with the exception of those on cyclical stocks, generally occurred because of bear markets. 

In a roaring bull market, don’t overlook a stock just because its P/E seems too high. It could be the next great winner. And never buy a stock just because the P/E ratio makes it look like a bargain. There are usually good reasons why the P/E is low, and there’s no golden rule that prevents a stock that sells at 8 or 10 times earnings from going even lower and selling at 4 or 5 times earnings. 

Many years ago, when I first began to study the market, I bought Northrop at 4 times earnings and watched in disbelief as the stock declined to a P/E ratio of 2. 

### How Price/Earnings Ratios Are Misused

许多华尔街分析师把一只股票列入“买入”名单，只因为它的市盈率处于历史区间的低端。他们还会在股价开始下跌时推荐一只股票，从而拉低市盈率，使它显得更划算。

1998 年，吉列和可口可乐看起来是很好的买点，因为股价回落了几个点、市盈率显得更具吸引力。实际上，两家公司的盈利都在明显减速，这正说明较低的估值是合理的。大量市盈率分析建立在个人意见和理论之上，由分析师、学者等人一代代传下来，而他们在市场上赚钱的记录既可疑、又无据可查。2008 年，一些华尔街分析师在美银一路下跌时一路推荐买入。市场中没有安全、确定无疑的事。这就是为什么你既需要买入规则，也需要回避或卖出规则。

依赖市盈率往往忽视更基本的趋势。例如，大盘可能已经见顶，那样所有股票都会走低。因为一家公司曾以 22 倍市盈率交易、如今 15 倍就能买到，就说它被低估，既荒唐又有些幼稚。

我有时确实会用市盈率，根据预估的未来盈利，来估算一只成长股未来 6 到 18 个月的潜在价格目标。我可能取未来两年的盈利预测，乘以该股在初始图表底部买点处的市盈率，再把结果乘以 100% 或略多一点。这就是一只成长股若有重大行情时，平均可能达到的市盈率扩张幅度。它告诉我，在牛市条件下，一只成长股潜在能卖到多高。不过，也有些牛市和某些成长股，市盈率可能几乎没有扩张。

例如，如果嘉信理财的股票以每股 43.75 美元突破其第一个底部（它 1998 年末确实如此），而其在初始买点处的市盈率为 40，那么把 40 乘以 130%，可看出若股价大涨，市盈率可能扩张到 92。接着，把潜在的市盈率 92 乘以两年后每股 1.45 美元的一致盈利预测。这就告诉你，你的成长股可能的价格目标。

Many Wall Street analysts put a stock on their “buy” list because it’s selling at the low end of its historical P/E range. They’ll also recommend a stock when the price starts to drop, thereby lowering the P/E and making it seem like an even bigger bargain. 

In 1998, Gillette and Coca-Cola looked like great buys because they had sold off several points and their P/Es looked more attractive. In actuality, the earnings at both companies were showing a material deceleration that justified a lower valuation. A great deal of P/E analysis is based on personal opinions and theories that have been handed down through the years by analysts, academicians, and others, whose track records when it comes to making money in the market are both questionable and undocumented. In 2008, some Wall Street analysts recommended buying Bank of America all the way down. There are no safe, sure things in the market. That’s why you need avoid or sell rules as well as buy rules. 

Reliance on P/E ratios often ignores more basic trends. The general market, for example, may have topped, in which case all stocks are headed lower. To say a company is undervalued because at one time it was selling at 22 times earnings and it can now be bought for 15 is ridiculous and somewhat naive. 

One way I do sometimes use P/E ratios is to estimate the potential price objective for a growth stock over the next 6 to 18 months based on its estimated future earnings. I may take the earnings estimate for the next two years and multiply it by the stock’s P/E ratio at the initial chart base buy point, then multiply the result by 100% or slightly more. This is the degree of P/E expansion possible on average if a growth stock has a major price move. This tells me what a growth stock could potentially sell for during bull market conditions. However, there are some bull markets and certain growth stocks that may have little or no P/E expansion. 

For example, if Charles Schwab’s stock breaks out of its first base at \$43.75 per share (as it did in late 1998) and its P/E ratio at the beginning buy point is 40, multiply 40 by 130% to see that the P/E ratio could possibly expand to 92 if the stock has a huge price move. Next, multiply the potential P/E ratio of 92 by the consensus earnings estimate two years out of \$1.45 per share. This tells you what a possible price objective for your growth stock might be. 

### The Wrong Way to Analyze Companies in an Industry

市盈率另一种被误用的方式，无论业余还是专业人士都有：评估某一行业中的股票，然后断定市盈率最便宜的那只总是被低估、因而最具吸引力。现实是，最低的市盈率通常属于盈利记录最糟糕的公司。

简单的事实是，在任何给定时刻，股票通常按其当前价值附近交易。以 20 倍市盈率卖出的股票，处于那个水平有一套原因；以 15 倍市盈率交易的股票，处于那个水平有另一套原因。一只以 7 倍市盈率卖出的股票之所以如此，是因为它的整体记录比市盈率更高的股票更差。此外请记住，周期股通常市盈率较低，而且即便在好时期，它们也不显示成长股那样的市盈率扩张。

你不可能用雪佛兰的价格买到奔驰，也不可能用内陆几英里土地的价格买到海滨地产。基于供需法则，一切东西大致都按其当时的价值出售。

许多年前，伟大画作的价值几乎单凭一位美术商约瑟夫·杜维恩（Joseph Duveen）之手而提升。他会去欧洲，以高于市价的价格买入伦勃朗等人的绝世孤品，然后带回美国，以远高于进价的价格卖给亨利·福特和那个时代其他实业家。换言之，杜维恩勋爵高价买入绝世珍品，再以高得多的价格卖出。

要点是：任何人都能以低价买一件平庸的艺术品，但最顶级的要贵得多。最顶级的股票，如同最顶级的艺术品，通常索价更高。

如果一家公司的价格和市盈率在不久的将来发生变化，那是因为条件、事件、心理和盈利在持续改善或开始恶化。最终，一只股票的市盈率会达到峰值，但这通常发生在大盘指数见顶、并开始显著下跌之时。它也可能是一个信号，表明公司的盈利增长率即将减弱。

诚然，高市盈率股票波动更大，尤其当它们属于高科技领域。高市盈率股票的价格也可能暂时跑在自身前面，但低市盈率股票同样如此。

Another faulty use of P/E ratios, by amateurs and professionals alike, is to evaluate the stocks in an industry and conclude the one selling at the cheapest P/E is always undervalued and therefore the most attractive purchase. The reality is, the lowest P/E usually belongs to the company with the most ghastly earnings record. 

The simple truth is that at any given time, stocks usually sell near their current value. The stock that sells at 20 times earnings is at that level for one set of reasons, and the stock that trades at 15 times earnings is at that level for another set of reasons. A stock selling at, say, 7 times earnings does so because its overall record is more deficient than that of a stock with a higher P/E ratio. Also, keep in mind that cyclical stocks normally have lower P/Es, and that, even in good periods, they do not show the P/E expansion that occurs in growth stocks. 

You can’t buy a Mercedes for the price of a Chevrolet, and you can’t buy oceanfront property for the same price you’d pay for land a couple of miles inland. Everything sells for about what it’s worth at the time based on the law of supply and demand. 

The increased value of great paintings was brought about almost singlehandedly many years ago by a fine-arts dealer named Joseph Duveen. He would travel to Europe and buy one-of-a-kind paintings by Rembrandt and others, paying more than the market price. He would then bring them back to the United States and sell them to Henry Ford and other industrialists of that era for substantially more than he had paid. In other words, Lord Duveen bought the one-of-a-kind masterpieces high and sold them much higher. 

The point is, anyone can buy a mediocre piece of art for a low price, but the very best costs more. The very best stocks, like the very best art, usually command a higher price. 

If a company’s price and P/E ratio change in the near future, it’s because conditions, events, psychology, and earnings have continued to improve or started to deteriorate. Eventually, a stock’s P/E will reach a peak, but this normally occurs when the general market averages are topping out and starting a significant decline. It could also be a signal the company’s rate of earnings growth is about to weaken. 

It’s true high-P/E stocks will be more volatile, particularly if they’re in the high-tech area. The price of a high-P/E stock can also temporarily get ahead of itself, but the same can be said for lower-P/E stocks. 

### Examples of High P/Es That Were Great Bargains

在小而有魅力、拥有革命性新产品的成长型公司身上，看似很高的市盈率实际上可能很低。例如：

• 施乐，1959 年推出首台干式复印机，1960 年以 100 倍市盈率卖出——随后股价上涨 3300%（从拆股调整后的 5 美元到 170 美元）。

• Syntex，首家为口服避孕药提交专利的公司，1963 年 7 月以 45 倍市盈率卖出——随后上涨 400%。

• 基因泰克，利用遗传信息开发新特效药的先驱、首家上市的生物技术公司，1985 年 11 月初始定价为 200 倍市盈率。五个月内，这只新股飙升 300%。

• 美国在线，其软件让数百万人进入革命性的互联网新世界，1994 年 11 月以 100 倍以上市盈率卖出，随后到 1999 年 12 月峰值时上涨 14900%。

• 谷歌的市盈率在 2004 年 9 月每股 115 美元时处于 50 到 60 多倍，一直到 2006 年 1 月初达到每股 475 美元。

事实是，对自认为的高市盈率抱有偏见的投资者，将错过当下或任何时代最伟大的机会。尤其在牛市中，这种偏见可能让你损失一笔财富。

In situations where small but captivating growth companies have revolutionary new products, what seems like a high P/E ratio can actually be low. For instance, 

• Xerox, which introduced the first dry copier in 1959, sold for 100 times earnings in 1960—before it advanced 3,300% in price (from a splitadjusted \$5 to \$170). 

• Syntex, the first company to submit a patent for a birth control pill, sold for 45 times earnings in July 1963—before it advanced 400%. 

• Genentech, a pioneer in the use of genetic information to develop new wonder drugs and the first biotech company to go public, was initially priced at 200 times earnings in November 1985. In five months, the new stock bolted 300%. 

• America Online, whose software gave millions access to the revolutionary new world of the Internet, sold for over 100 times earnings in November 1994 before climbing 14,900% to its peak in December 1999. 

• Google’s P/E was in the 50s and 60s from \$115 a share in September 2004 until it hit \$475 a share in early January 2006. 

The fact is, investors with a bias against what they consider to be high P/Es will miss out on some of the greatest opportunities of this or any other time. During bull markets, in particular, such a bias could literally cost you a fortune. 

### Don’t Sell High-P/E Stocks Short

1962 年 6 月，股市处于谷底时，比弗利山庄一位大投资者冲进我一位经纪人朋友的办公室，大声嚷嚷说，以 50 倍市盈率计算，施乐被严重高估。他随即以 88 美元卖空 2000 股（向经纪人借入股票卖出，指望股价下跌、日后以更低价格买回，从而赚取差价）。果然，股票立刻起飞，最终涨到 1300 美元（未做拆股调整），市盈率一度超过 80。

关于市盈率太高的种种意见，就此打住！投资者个人的意见通常是错的，市场则几乎总是对的。所以，别再和市场较劲、争辩了。

专注于那些过去三年每年都有显著盈利增长记录、且近期季度盈利大幅改善的股票。不要接受更差的标准。

In June 1962, when the stock market was at rock bottom, a big Beverly Hills investor barged into the office of a broker friend of mine and shouted that, at 50 times earnings, Xerox was drastically overpriced. He proceeded to sell 

2,000 shares short at \$88 (borrowing stock from his broker to sell in hopes the stock would decline and he could later buy it back cheaper, making money on the difference in price). Sure enough, the stock took off at once and ultimately reached a price of \$1,300 (before adjusting for splits) with a P/E ratio that topped 80. 

So much for opinions about P/Es being too high! Investors’ personal opinions are usually wrong; the market is almost always right. So stop fighting and arguing with the market. 

Concentrate on stocks with proven records of significant earnings growth in each of the last three years plus strong recent quarterly improvements. Don’t accept anything less.
